Storyboard & Core Assumptions

Foundation

Assumptions

Core design assumptions and hypotheses guiding our rapid prototyping trajectory:

01

Mobile-Friendly Interface • Progressive Complexity

Assumption: A mobile-friendly interface is possible and optimal for field operations.

Leveraging the UX framework of progressive complexity: we began with mobile-first constraints to prioritize core actions and facilitate fast prototyping, before expanding functionality outward to full desktop control centers.

02

Industry Readiness for Unified Automation

Assumption: The highways maintenance industry is ready for automated vehicle repairs.

Assuming that disparate multi-domain services—smart self-healing materials, autonomous robotic crack sealers, and predictive AI data science—can be unified into a single, cohesive product ecosystem.

Experience Learned • C4 Model

To resolve communication silos across robotics, smart materials, data science, and digital twin teams, I adopted the C4 Architecture Model to formalize system boundaries and standardize inputs and outputs before writing glue code:

1 • Context
Highways maintenance ecosystem, partner contractors & external APIs
2 • Container
Web app, PostGIS DB, Unreal Engine twin & autonomous repair platforms
3 • Component
Task Dispatcher, Triage Engine, GIS microservice & User Services
4 • Code
Defect classification algorithms, API schemas & state machine handlers
02 • Algorithmic Intelligence

Data Science Treatment Engine

Algorithmic mapping between detected highway defects, root cause, and treatment recommendations.

Inspect Full Res
1. Highway Defects Transverse, longitudinal & alligator cracking, ravelling, rutting, road marking degradation, and drainage failure.
2. Root Causes Heavy traffic axle loading, moisture penetration, aggregate design mix aging, thermal contraction, and soil subsidence.
3. Treatment Strategies Preventative sealing, lane speed reduction, asphalt mix redesign, isolation, and scheduled structural resurfacing.
  • Decision Automation: Feeds qualified treatment recommendations directly into task order generation for human review or robotic dispatch.

Opportunity Solution Tree: Full Feature Specifications

User and stakeholder priority results across continuous discovery categories

Maintenance planning & prioritisation

  • Driver-based prioritisation: Improve the ability to prioritise repairs based on safety, urgency, operational constraints, available resources, and proactive lifecycle planning.
  • Deterioration prediction: Use academic research findings to develop deterioration predictions that support repair planning.
  • Repair route optimisation: Optimise service provider repair routes and resource allocation by integrating Rui’s PhD research.
  • Reactive & proactive coordination: Improve communication between reactive and proactive maintenance teams through a shared interface, helping reduce the cost of expensive reactive maintenance.

Visualisation and UX

  • Defect view: Improve defect visualisation through maps, defect polygons, extracted imagery, and 360° images that provide location and surrounding context and support road closure arrangements.
  • Human in the loop: Provide a human-in-the-loop interface for verifying low-confidence road defect data from computer vision and other data acquisition sources.
  • Repair history view: Provide transparent visualisation of repair history for a road section.
  • Jobs and invoices: Integrate visualisation with job and invoicing systems to clarify repair tasks and support completion checks.
  • Team review & process standardisation: Enable users to request team opinions on decisions, helping to standardise decision-making across teams and processes.

RoadGP Specific

  • Costs and treatment advice: Integrate RoadGP with costing systems to support cost-effective repair planning when cost is a key decision driver.
  • Failed repairs input: Collect information about failed repairs through the interface to improve future decision-making.
  • Issue data input UI: Provide clear and structured issue data-input interfaces to improve RoadGP recommendations and decision support.
  • Clustering automation: Automate defect clustering to create assessment segments for RoadGP treatment advice.
  • Clusters, site labels & repair planning segments: Connect RoadGP defect clusters at 15 m, National Highways network-model segments at 100 m, and repair job areas at 10 km through the interface.

Security and Governance

  • Governance and security: Define integration protocols and secure cloud architecture for connecting the interface with National Highways enterprise systems.

Quantitative comparison matrix and strategic scoring rationale for Digital Roads candidate outputs

RICE Prioritisation Formula
RICE Score = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort
Standardized 1–4 scale: Reach (1=Local • 4=National Network), Impact (1=Minor • 4=Transformative), Confidence (1=Low/Early • 4=High/Tested Evidence), Effort (1=Rapid Build • 4=Heavy Multi-Year R&D).
